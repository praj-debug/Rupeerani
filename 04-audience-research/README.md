# Audience Research

Research real people and real questions before locking the content strategy. Protect private information and publish aggregated insights rather than identifiable interview details.

## Research questions

- What money decisions are people making in the next 3 to 12 months?
- Which terms, fees, risks or processes are hard to understand?
- Where do they currently learn about money, and which sources do they trust?
- Which formats and languages make explanations easier to use?
- What makes financial content feel unsafe, sales-driven or confusing?

## Starting audience hypotheses

1. Young adults may need practical support with first salary, budgeting and saving.
2. Early-career professionals may need clearer explanations of credit cards, credit scores, EMIs and borrowing costs.
3. Beginners interested in investing may benefit from foundational concepts, risk explanations and scam awareness before product comparisons.

Validate each hypothesis. Do not present hypotheses as research findings.

## Interview template

1. Tell me about the last money decision you had to make.
2. What did you find confusing or difficult?
3. Where did you search for answers?
4. What made a source feel trustworthy or untrustworthy?
5. What did you do next, and why?
6. What would have made the decision easier?

Ask about behaviour and past examples rather than leading the person toward a preferred answer.

## Research ethics

Collect the minimum information needed. Obtain consent, anonymise notes, avoid storing account numbers or sensitive financial details, and allow participants to decline questions. Public content should not expose a person's financial situation.
